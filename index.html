Plasmie

A small pink blob that lives beside your MacBook’s notch. It keeps notes on your work day and answers questions about them. Everything runs on your Mac.

Download for Mac

Version 0.11.0. Needs an Apple silicon Mac on macOS 26 or later, with Apple Intelligence turned on.

What it does with your day

Plasmie works in the background on a schedule. You don’t have to open it for any of this.

  1. Gathers

    Every hour

    Reads new activity from the sources you switch on: Slack, GitHub, Confluence, Outlook mail and calendar, commits in your local git repos, and Claude Code sessions. Passwords, keys and tokens are stripped out before anything is saved.

  2. Summarises

    Every hour

    Writes a short digest of what happened, using Apple’s on-device model.

  3. Files

    Overnight, around 2:30 am

    Turns what’s worth keeping into tagged notes. If it isn’t confident about something, it holds it back for you to approve instead of filing a guess.

  4. Answers

    When you press ⌃⌥⌘K

    Ask about your work from any app. The answer comes only from your notes, and lists the notes it used so you can open them.

The shape is the status

Plasmie shows its status by changing shape, so one look at the notch tells you whether things are working.

  • Hand up

    Some notes are waiting for your OK.

  • Puddle

    A connector stopped working.

  • Hourglass

    Your Mac is hot or busy, so background jobs wait.

  • Flat and asleep

    Nothing new has come in for a while.

  • Grey ball

    Every connector is switched off.

Your notes stay on your Mac

Plasmie reads your work, so here is what it sends and where.

The AI runs on your Mac
Summaries, filing and answers all use Apple’s on-device model. Apple’s Private Cloud Compute is never used, and no other AI service is called.
No tracking
No analytics, no telemetry, no crash reports. Nothing is sent automatically.
You pick the sources
Connectors that use the network start off. Each one you switch on talks only to its own service, to read your own account. One that’s off makes no network calls.
One download at setup
The search engine and its models, about 4 GB, downloaded once. After that, the only network use is the connectors you switch on, and iCloud if you turn it on.
Sign-ins live in Keychain
Tokens and passwords go in your Mac’s login Keychain, never in a plain file.
You can check it
Every prompt sent to the model is logged on your Mac, so you can see exactly what it read.
iCloud is optional
Off unless you turn it on. Then your filed notes and daily digests are copied to iCloud Drive to read on an iPhone or iPad. Raw captures, like your Slack messages, are never copied.

Your notes are plain Markdown files

They live in a folder you pick, ~/knowledge unless you choose another. Read and edit them in Plasmie, or open the same folder in Obsidian if you use it.

Point it at notes you already have and Plasmie adds what it needs next to them. It searches your own notes but never moves or rewrites them on its own.

The map draws your notes as a graph and suggests links between related ones. Suggestions stay out of your files unless you ask Plasmie to add them.

To remove Plasmie, Reset Plasmie clears its background jobs, settings and sign-ins. Your notes folder is never touched.

Install

You need

  • A Mac with Apple silicon (M1 or newer)
  • macOS 26 or later
  • Apple Intelligence turned on, in System Settings › Apple Intelligence & Siri
  • About 6 GB of free space, and internet for the first setup

Steps

  1. Open Plasmie.dmg and drag Plasmie into Applications.

  2. Open Plasmie. macOS says it can’t check the app because it isn’t from the App Store. Click Done.

  3. In System Settings › Privacy & Security, click Open Anyway next to Plasmie. You only do this once.

  4. Plasmie checks your Mac, asks where to keep your notes, and sets itself up. You can keep working while it does.
